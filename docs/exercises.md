### Bodyweight squat

`bodyweight_squat` · Lower body · orientation: standing · anchor: feet · rep: 4.4 s · equipment: none

The air squat: hips and knees flex together to a deep position with the trunk inclined ~35 deg, then the hips and knees extend to standing.

**Setup**
- Feet shoulder-width, toes turned out 10-30 deg
- Arms forward as a counterbalance
- Brace the trunk; look ahead

**Phases** (pose reached at the end of each; joint motion from the previous)

| phase | kind | s | moving joints | cues |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Descent | eccentric | 2.00 | Hips: flexion 0° → 120°; Knees: flexion 0° → 120°; Ankles: dorsiflexion 0° → 35°; trunk pitch +35° | Sit the hips back and down; knees track over the toes Chest up, spine neutral, weight over mid-foot |
| Bottom | isometric | 0.40 | trunk pitch +35° | Thighs at or below parallel; heels down |
| Ascent | concentric | 1.50 | Hips: extension 120° → 0°; Knees: extension 120° → 0°; Ankles: plantarflexion 35° → 0° | Drive the floor away; hips and chest rise together Push the knees out as you stand |
| Lockout | isometric | 0.50 | hold | Stand tall, squeeze the glutes, breathe |

**Muscles**
- *Primary movers:* Quadriceps 95 %; Gluteus maximus 75 %
- *Synergists:* Hip adductors 60 % — adductor magnus extends the hip out of the hole; Hamstrings 40 %; Soleus 40 %; Gastrocnemius 30 %
- *Stabilisers:* Erector spinae 55 %; Rectus abdominis 30 %; Obliques 30 %; Gluteus medius / minimus 35 % — keeps the knees tracking over the toes; Tibialis anterior 25 %

**Common errors**
- Knee valgus (knees caving in): cue 'knees out over the toes'; turning the feet out ~30 deg halves the valgus moment.
- Heels lifting: the ankle needs ~35 deg dorsiflexion for a deep squat; raise the heels or limit depth.
- Butt wink (posterior pelvic tilt) below the available hip flexion: stop at the depth the hips allow.
- Excessive forward lean with lumbar flexion: brace and keep the chest up; a rounded lumbar spine tolerates compression poorly.

**Physiotherapy notes**
- Patellofemoral stress rises steadily from 0 to 90 deg of knee flexion; shallow depths for anterior knee pain, full depth is tolerated by healthy knees.
- Gluteus maximus activity increases ~65 % from partial to parallel depth.

**Sources**

- A biomechanical review of the squat exercise: implications for clinical practice, IJSPT (depth by knee flexion 0-90 / 90-110 / 110-135 deg; gluteus maximus 28-35 %MVIC) https://ijspt.scholasticahq.com/article/94600

### Box squat

`box_squat` · Lower body · orientation: standing · anchor: feet · rep: 4.8 s · equipment: barbell, plyo box

A back squat to a box, sitting back onto it and pausing before standing. The box fixes the depth and the pause removes the bounce.

**Setup**
- Box set so the crease of the hip finishes at or just below the knee
- Sit back rather than down: shins stay near vertical
- Pause on the box without relaxing the trunk, then drive up

**Phases** (pose reached at the end of each; joint motion from the previous)

| phase | kind | s | moving joints | cues |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Sit back | eccentric | 2.20 | Hips: flexion 0° → 105°; Knees: flexion 0° → 95°; Ankles: dorsiflexion 0° → 28°; trunk pitch +38° | Push the hips back to the box; keep the shins vertical |
| Pause | isometric | 0.80 | trunk pitch +38° | Sit, do not slump: stay braced and keep the weight on the feet |
| Drive | concentric | 1.40 | Hips: extension 105° → 0°; Knees: extension 95° → 0°; Ankles: plantarflexion 28° → 0° | Chest and hips rise together off the box |
| Lockout | isometric | 0.40 | hold |  |

**Muscles**
- *Primary movers:* Quadriceps 95 %; Gluteus maximus 75 %; Erector spinae 75 % — holds the trunk through the dead stop
- *Synergists:* Hip adductors 60 % — adductor magnus extends the hip out of the hole; Hamstrings 40 %; Soleus 40 %; Gastrocnemius 30 %
- *Stabilisers:* Erector spinae 55 %; Rectus abdominis 30 %; Obliques 30 %; Gluteus medius / minimus 35 % — keeps the knees tracking over the toes; Tibialis anterior 25 %

**Common errors**
- Rocking backwards on the box and losing the brace.
- Dropping onto it rather than sitting back under control.
- Letting the shins travel forward, which turns it into an ordinary squat.

**Physiotherapy notes**
- Sitting back with vertical shins increases the hip contribution and reduces the knee's, which is why it is a common posterior-chain squat variant.
- The pause removes the stretch-shortening contribution, so the drive starts from a dead stop.

### Pause squat

`pause_squat` · Lower body · orientation: standing · anchor: feet · rep: 6.7 s · equipment: barbell

A back squat held for two to three seconds at the bottom. The pause kills the bounce and exposes any position that was being hidden by speed.

**Setup**
- Set up as for a back squat
- Descend under control to full depth
- Hold without relaxing, then drive: the brace must not change

**Phases** (pose reached at the end of each; joint motion from the previous)

| phase | kind | s | moving joints | cues |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Descent | eccentric | 2.20 | Knees: flexion 0° → 125°; Hips: flexion 0° → 120°; Ankles: dorsiflexion 0° → 37°; trunk pitch +32° | Controlled descent to depth |
| Pause | isometric | 2.50 | trunk pitch +32° | Stay tight: knees out, chest up, weight over mid-foot Do not relax into the bottom |
| Ascent | concentric | 1.60 | Knees: extension 125° → 0°; Hips: extension 120° → 0°; Ankles: plantarflexion 37° → 0° | Drive from a dead stop; hips and chest together |
| Lockout | isometric | 0.40 | hold |  |

**Muscles**
- *Primary movers:* Quadriceps 95 %; Gluteus maximus 75 %; Erector spinae 80 % — the pause is its test
- *Synergists:* Hip adductors 60 % — adductor magnus extends the hip out of the hole; Hamstrings 40 %; Soleus 40 %; Gastrocnemius 30 %
- *Stabilisers:* Erector spinae 55 %; Rectus abdominis 30 %; Obliques 30 %; Gluteus medius / minimus 35 % — keeps the knees tracking over the toes; Tibialis anterior 25 %

**Common errors**

- Letting the chest drop through the pause.

### Conventional deadlift

`conventional_deadlift` · Lower body · orientation: standing · anchor: feet · rep: 4.8 s · equipment: barbell

A hip-dominant lift from the floor: the trunk starts ~55 deg from vertical with the hips above the knees, and knees and hips extend together to lockout.

**Setup**
- Bar over mid-foot, shins ~2-3 cm from the bar
- Hip-width stance, grip just outside the legs
- Chest up, lats engaged, neutral spine, hips higher than the knees

**Phases** (pose reached at the end of each; joint motion from the previous)

| phase | kind | s | moving joints | cues |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Pull to the knee | concentric | 1.00 | Knees: extension 110° → 30°; Hips: extension 125° → 62°; Ankles: plantarflexion 43° → 8°; Shoulders: extension 58° → 40°; trunk pitch +40° | Push the floor away; hips and shoulders rise at the same rate Bar stays in contact with the shins |
| Lockout | concentric | 0.80 | Hips: extension 62° → 0°; Shoulders: extension 40° → 3°; Knees: extension 30° → 3°; Ankles: plantarflexion 8° → 0° | Drive the hips through; stand tall without leaning back |
| Top | isometric | 0.50 | hold | Squeeze the glutes; do not hyperextend |
| Lower to the knee | eccentric | 1.00 | Hips: flexion 0° → 62°; Shoulders: flexion 3° → 40°; Knees: flexion 3° → 30°; Ankles: dorsiflexion 0° → 8°; trunk pitch +40° | Hinge the hips back first; bar slides down the thighs |
| Lower to the floor | eccentric | 1.00 | Knees: flexion 30° → 110°; Hips: flexion 62° → 125°; Ankles: dorsiflexion 8° → 43°; Shoulders: flexion 40° → 58°; trunk pitch +58° | Bend the knees once the bar passes them |
| Reset | isometric | 0.50 | trunk pitch +58° | Neutral spine, lats tight, take the slack out of the bar |

**Muscles**
- *Primary movers:* Gluteus maximus 95 %; Hamstrings 80 %; Erector spinae 90 % — isometric hold against flexion
- *Synergists:* Quadriceps 60 %; Hip adductors 50 %; Tibialis anterior 50 % — 82 %MVC peak in the first pull
- *Stabilisers:* Latissimus dorsi / teres major 50 % — keeps the bar close; Upper trapezius 50 %; Middle trapezius 40 %; Forearm flexors (grip) 70 % — grip; Rectus abdominis 40 %; Obliques 40 %; Gluteus medius / minimus 30 %

**Common errors**
- Hips shooting up first, turning the lift into a stiff-leg pull: keep the chest and hips rising together.
- Rounding the lumbar spine: brace and pull the slack out before the bar leaves the floor.
- Bar drifting away from the legs.
- Hyperextending at lockout.

**Physiotherapy notes**
- Biceps femoris peaks at ~78 %MVC and vastus lateralis ~55 % in the first pull.
- The more horizontal the trunk, the higher the spinal extensor demand; the sumo stance is 5-9 deg more upright.

### Sumo deadlift

`sumo_deadlift` · Lower body · orientation: standing · anchor: feet · rep: 4.8 s · equipment: barbell

A wide stance with the feet turned out puts the trunk more upright and shares the work between hips, adductors and quadriceps.

**Setup**

- Grip inside the knees

### Rack pull

`rack_pull` · Lower body · orientation: standing · anchor: feet · rep: 3.7 s · equipment: barbell

A deadlift started from pins at about knee height. The bottom third is gone, which leaves the lockout and lets the load go up.

**Setup**
- Pins set so the bar starts at or just below the knee

### Barbell bench press

`barbell_bench_press` · Upper body: push · orientation: supine · anchor: none · rep: 4.3 s · equipment: barbell, bench

Supine on a bench, the bar is lowered to the lower sternum with the elbows at ~45 deg from the torso and pressed back over the shoulders.

**Setup**
- Eyes under the bar, five points of contact (head, shoulders, hips, both feet)
- Grip ~1.5x shoulder width, wrists stacked over the elbows
- Shoulder blades pulled back and down; slight arch, ribs up

**Phases** (pose reached at the end of each; joint motion from the previous)

| phase | kind | s | moving joints | cues |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Lower | eccentric | 2.00 | Shoulders: extension 75° → 15°; Elbows: flexion 10° → 80°; Shoulders: abduction 12° → 75°; Forearms: supination 0° → 60° | Bar to the lower sternum, elbows ~45 deg from the trunk Forearms vertical Pull the bar down with the lats; blades stay back and down |

| Press | concentric | 1.50 | Shoulders: flexion 15° → 75°; Elbows: extension 80° → 10°; Shoulders: adduction 75° → 12°; Forearms: pronation 60° → 0° | Drive the bar up and slightly back over the shoulders Push the feet into the floor Keep the upper back tight against the bench |
| Lockout | isometric | 0.50 | hold | Elbows straight, shoulder blades down |

**Muscles**
- *Primary movers:* Pectoralis major 90 %; Anterior deltoid 75 %; Triceps brachii 70 %
- *Synergists:* Serratus anterior 40 %
- *Stabilisers:* Latissimus dorsi / teres major 45 % — pulls the bar down under control and holds the shoulder tight off the chest; Rhomboids 40 % — holds the blades retracted; Middle trapezius 35 % — holds the blades retracted; Erector spinae 30 % — braces the trunk against the bench; Rotator cuff 35 %; Biceps brachii 20 %; Forearm flexors (grip) 45 %; Gluteus maximus 30 % — leg drive; Quadriceps 25 %

- Elbows flared to 90 deg (raises impingement risk and shoulder strain).
- Bouncing the bar off the chest.
- Feet lifting or hips leaving the bench.
- Wrists bent back under the bar.

- Pectoralis major dominates the bottom of the lift; triceps and anterior deltoid carry the mid and upper range.

- The back is not passive. `pendlay_row` · Upper body: pull · orientation: standing · anchor: feet · rep: 2.8 s · equipment: barbell

A barbell row from a dead stop on the floor with the trunk parallel to it. Every rep starts from rest, so there is no stretch to use and no swing.

**Setup**
- Trunk parallel to the floor, bar over the mid-foot
- Back flat and held there: the trunk angle does not change
- Pull to the lower sternum and return the bar to the floor

**Phases** (pose reached at the end of each; joint motion from the previous)

| phase | kind | s | moving joints | cues |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Pull | concentric | 1.00 | Elbows: flexion 8° → 110°; Shoulders: abduction 8° → 25°; Shoulders: extension 88° → 80°; trunk pitch +85° | Explode the bar to the lower sternum with the trunk still |
| Squeeze | isometric | 0.30 | trunk pitch +85° | Blades together; do not let the chest drop |
| Lower | eccentric | 1.00 | Elbows: extension 110° → 8°; Shoulders: adduction 25° → 8°; Shoulders: flexion 80° → 88°; trunk pitch +85° | Put it back on the floor; do not lower it slowly and hover |
| Dead stop | isometric | 0.50 | trunk pitch +85° | Let it rest: the next rep starts from nothing |

- *Primary movers:* Latissimus dorsi / teres major 90 %; Rhomboids 80 %; Middle trapezius 80 %; Erector spinae 80 % — holds the parallel trunk for every rep

- *Stabilisers:* Hamstrings 55 %; Gluteus maximus 50 %; Forearm flexors (grip) 60 %; Rectus abdominis 45 %

- Raising the trunk to help the bar up: the angle is the exercise.
- Turning it into a bent-over row by never touching the floor.
- Rounding the back to reach the bar at the start.

**Physiotherapy notes**
- The dead stop removes the stretch-shortening contribution and the cheat, which is why the loads are lower than a bent-over row's and the upper back does more of the work.

### Push jerk

`push_jerk` · Athletic and power · orientation: standing · anchor: feet · rep: 2.1 s · equipment: barbell

From the front rack, a dip and drive sends the bar off the shoulders and the lifter drops under it into a quarter squat to catch it locked overhead. More than a push press: the feet stay in place but the body moves down to meet the bar.

**Setup**
- Bar in the front rack, elbows up, feet under the hips
- Dip a hand's width with the trunk vertical, then drive
- Punch the head through and receive it with the knees bent

**Phases** (pose reached at the end of each; joint motion from the previous)

|---|---|---|---|---|
| Dip | eccentric | 0.35 | trunk pitch +4° | Short, vertical, heels down |
| Drive | concentric | 0.20 | Shoulders: flexion 90° → 150°; Ankles: plantarflexion 9° → 20°; Elbows: extension 145° → 120°; Knees: extension 25° → 4° | Extend hard; the bar leaves the shoulders on the legs |
| Drop under | eccentric | 0.22 | Elbows: extension 120° → 5°; Hips: flexion 0° → 55°; Knees: flexion 4° → 55°; Forearms: pronation 0° → 30°; trunk pitch +8° | Punch under it: arms lock as the hips drop |

**Muscles**
- *Primary movers:* Anterior deltoid 90 %; Triceps brachii 85 %; Quadriceps 85 % — the dip and drive; Gluteus maximus 75 %; Erector spinae 70 % — holds the trunk vertical

### Muscle-up (bar)

`muscle_up` · Calisthenics and bodyweight skill · orientation: hanging · anchor: hands · rep: 5.0 s · equipment: pullup bar

A pull-up that continues into a dip: the chest clears the bar, the wrists roll over it and the arms press the body up to a straight-arm support. The transition is the whole exercise.

**Setup**
- False grip if you have it: a wrist already over the bar saves the transition
- Pull higher than a pull-up -- sternum to the bar, not the chin
- Lean the chest forward over the bar as the elbows come through

**Phases** (pose reached at the end of each; joint motion from the previous)

| phase | kind | s | moving joints | cues |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Pull | concentric | 0.90 | Elbows: flexion 5° → 145°; Shoulders: adduction 165° → 45°; Shoulders: internal rotation 90° → 30°; Shoulders: extension 10° → 45° | Pull explosively; sternum to the bar, not the chin Elbows down and back, shoulder blades depressed |
| Transition | transition | 0.60 | Elbows: extension 145° → 120°; Hips: flexion 10° → 25°; Shoulders: extension 45° → 60°; Knees: extension 30° → 25° | Lean the chest forward over the bar and whip the elbows through This is where the rep is won or lost |
| Press | concentric | 0.80 | Shoulders: flexion 60° → 20°; Elbows: extension 120° → 85°; Knees: flexion 25° → 60°; Forearms: supination 45° → 25° | Press out of the bottom of the dip |
| Support | isometric | 0.50 | Elbows: extension 85° → 0°; Shoulders: flexion 20° → 15°; Forearms: supination 25° → 0°; Shoulders: adduction 25° → 10° | Locked out above the bar, shoulders down away from the ears |
| Reverse the transition | eccentric | 0.80 | Elbows: flexion 0° → 120°; Shoulders: extension 15° → 60°; Forearms: pronation 0° → 45°; Knees: extension 70° → 25° | Lean back over the bar and let the elbows come back up |
| Lower | eccentric | 1.40 | Shoulders: abduction 45° → 165°; Elbows: extension 120° → 5°; Shoulders: flexion 60° → 10°; Shoulders: external rotation 30° → 90° | Reverse it under control: back through the transition to the hang |

**Muscles**
- *Primary movers:* Latissimus dorsi / teres major 100 %; Pectoralis major 85 %; Triceps brachii 85 % — the dip half; Biceps brachii 80 %; Rectus abdominis 70 % — holds the body against the swing; Forearm flexors (grip) 85 % — the false grip
- *Synergists:* Anterior deltoid 70 %; Lower trapezius 60 %; Rhomboids 60 %; Serratus anterior 60 %; Brachialis 60 %; Obliques 55 %
- *Stabilisers:* Rotator cuff 50 %

**Common errors**
- Kipping so hard the transition becomes a swing the shoulders have to catch.
- Pulling only to the chin: the chest has to clear the bar.
- Attempting it before a strict pull-up and a bar dip are both easy.

**Physiotherapy notes**
- The usual prerequisite is a strict pull-up to the sternum and a clean bar dip; the transition loads the shoulder at end-range internal rotation, which is why it is taught after both.

`l_sit` · Calisthenics and bodyweight skill · orientation: hanging · anchor: hands · rep: 7.8 s · equipment: dip station

Supported on straight arms with the legs held horizontal, so the body makes an L. A hold whose difficulty is the lever: the straighter the legs, the harder it is.

### Nordic hamstring curl

`nordic_hamstring_curl` · Calisthenics and bodyweight skill · orientation: prone · anchor: feet · rep: 6.2 s · equipment: mat

### Standing calf stretch (wall)

`calf_stretch` · Yoga, stretches and mobility · orientation: standing · anchor: feet · rep: 34.5 s · equipment: wall

A split stance with the hands on a wall and the back heel pressed down. Straight back knee lengthens gastrocnemius; bending it shifts the stretch to soleus.

|---|---|---|---|---|

### Supine figure-four (gluteal / piriformis stretch)

`supine_figure_four` · Yoga, stretches and mobility · orientation: supine · anchor: none · rep: 25.8 s · equipment: mat

Lying on the back, one ankle crosses the opposite thigh and that thigh is drawn in: hip flexion with external rotation lengthens the deep rotators and gluteus medius.

**Setup**
- Lie on the back, both knees bent
- Cross one ankle over the opposite thigh above the knee
- Reach through and draw the supporting thigh toward the chest

**Phases** (pose reached at the end of each; joint motion from the previous)

| phase | kind | s | moving joints | cues |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Cross and draw in | concentric | 2.50 | Elbows: flexion 20° → 105°; Shoulders: flexion 25° → 100°; Hip (L): flexion 45° → 95°; Hip (R): external rotation 0° → 42° | Let the crossed knee fall out; keep that foot flexed to protect the knee Draw the far thigh in until you feel it in the buttock |
| Hold | isometric | 20.00 | hold | Keep the head and the opposite shoulder down; breathe |
| Release | eccentric | 1.80 | Elbows: extension 105° → 20°; Shoulders: extension 100° → 25°; Hip (L): extension 95° → 45°; Hip (R): internal rotation 42° → 0° | Uncross and put both feet down |
| Rest | transition | 1.50 | hold |  |

**Muscles**
- *Primary movers:* Deep hip rotators (R) 85 % — the target, piriformis among them; Gluteus maximus (R) 80 % — lengthened; Gluteus medius / minimus (R) 75 % — the posterior fibres
- *Synergists:* TFL / IT band (R) 50 % — lengthened; Hip adductors (R) 45 %; Hip flexors (iliopsoas) (L) 50 % — works to hold the thigh in; Hamstrings (L) 45 % — lengthened at the hip
- *Stabilisers:* Biceps brachii 40 %; Rectus abdominis 35 %

**Common errors**
- Letting the crossed foot go floppy, which puts the strain into the knee.
- Lifting the head and the opposite shoulder off the floor.
- Forcing the crossed knee down with a hand.

**Physiotherapy notes**
- Piriformis is an external rotator with the hip extended and an abductor with it flexed past ~60 deg, which is why the stretch position combines flexion, adduction and external rotation.

- Page P 2012. Current concepts in muscle stretching for exercise and rehabilitation. IJSPT 7(1):109
- Neumann DA 2017. Kinesiology of the Musculoskeletal System, 3rd ed.

### Supine spinal twist (supta matsyendrasana)

`supine_spinal_twist` · Yoga, stretches and mobility · orientation: supine · anchor: none · rep: 26.5 s · equipment: mat

Lying on the back with the arms out to the sides, one knee is drawn across the body toward the floor: rotation for the trunk with both shoulders staying down.

**Setup**
- Lie on the back, arms out to the sides at shoulder height
- Draw one knee up and let it fall across the body
- Keep both shoulders on the floor -- that is what limits the twist, not the knee

**Phases** (pose reached at the end of each; joint motion from the previous)

| phase | kind | s | moving joints | cues |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Cross over | eccentric | 3.00 | Knee (L): extension 70° → 8°; Hip (R): flexion 35° → 85°; Shoulders: abduction 50° → 82°; Hip (L): extension 35° → 5° | Let the knee fall; do not push it down with the hand Turn the head the other way if the neck is comfortable |
| Hold | isometric | 20.00 | hold | Breathe out and let the shoulder settle back toward the floor |
| Return | concentric | 2.00 | Knee (L): flexion 8° → 70°; Hip (R): extension 85° → 35°; Shoulders: adduction 82° → 50°; Hip (L): flexion 5° → 35° | Bring the knee back to the middle |
| Rest | transition | 1.50 | hold |  |

**Muscles**
- *Primary movers:* Obliques 80 % — the target: the external oblique of the turning side; Erector spinae 70 % — the thoracolumbar rotators, lengthened; Gluteus medius / minimus (R) 70 % — lengthened as the hip adducts across the body
- *Synergists:* Quadratus lumborum 60 % — lengthened; TFL / IT band (R) 55 %; Multifidus / rotatores 50 %; Latissimus dorsi / teres major 50 %; Pectoralis major 50 % — lengthened with the arms out
- *Stabilisers:* Rhomboids 45 % — works to keep the shoulder down; Hip flexors (iliopsoas) 40 %

**Common errors**
- Pressing the knee down with the hand until the opposite shoulder lifts.
- Holding the breath: the exhale is what lets the ribcage turn.
- Twisting hard through an irritable lumbar spine rather than letting it settle.

**Physiotherapy notes**
- The shoulder staying down is the criterion; once it lifts, the rotation has moved from the trunk into the shoulder girdle and nothing further is being stretched.

- Neumann DA 2017. Kinesiology of the Musculoskeletal System, 3rd ed.
