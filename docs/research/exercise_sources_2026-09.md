# Research notes for the exercise catalogue (September 2026)

What was consulted to author the exercise definitions, and how much of each
number is measured versus received. The session's web-search budget was
exhausted part-way (four delegated research agents were cut off by a rate
limit before doing any work), so the numeric grounding below comes from six
searches and six fetched papers; everything else is standard reference
material cited by name and marked as such in the catalogue sources.

## Fetched and used

| topic | finding used | source |
|---|---|---|
| Deep back squat depth | Hip and knee *relative* angles 51–57° (≈125° flexion) at maximum depth across 60 %–100 % 1RM in 17 powerlifters/weightlifters; depth barely changes with load | Kinematic analysis of the back squat at different load intensities, Front Sports Act Living 2024 — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11565377/ |
| Squat depth bands, clinical notes | Partial 0–90°, parallel 90–110°, deep 110–135° knee flexion; gluteus maximus 28–35 %MVIC, +65 % from partial to parallel; feet out 30° halves the knee valgus moment; patellofemoral stress rises 0–90°; depth beyond available hip flexion causes posterior pelvic tilt | A biomechanical review of the squat exercise: implications for clinical practice, IJSPT — https://ijspt.scholasticahq.com/article/94600 |
| Conventional vs sumo deadlift | Phase-1 (liftoff to knee) ROM hip 38°, knee 33°, ankle 13°; biceps femoris 78 %MVC and tibialis anterior 82 % conventional; vastus lateralis 55 % (conventional) vs 63 % (sumo) | Biomechanical analysis of conventional and sumo deadlift, 2025 — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12148905/ |
| Bench press position and EMG | Bar 1 cm from the sternum with shoulders abducted ~45°, grip 150 % biacromial, 2 s / 2 s tempo, 60 %1RM; pectoralis ~27 %MVIC flat, anterior deltoid 26→33 % with incline, triceps ~15 % at all inclines; upper pectoralis peaks at 30° incline | Effect of five bench inclinations on EMG of pectoralis major, anterior deltoid and triceps, 2020 — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7579505/ |
| Pull-up / chin-up | Latissimus 117–130 %, biceps 78–96 %, lower trapezius 45–56 % of the reference MVIC in chin-ups; initiated by lower trapezius and pectoralis major, completed by biceps and latissimus; pronated grip → more lower trapezius, supinated → more pectoralis and biceps | Youdas JW et al. 2010, JSCR 24(12):3404 (via search summary and thesis citing it) |
| Lat pull-down front vs back | Front variant: greater latissimus activation in the descending phase; back: greater in the ascending; no explicit joint angles | High-density EMG in front vs back lat pull-down — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11057623/ |
| Eccentric vs concentric EMG | 7–31 % lower EMG in eccentric than velocity-matched concentric actions → the 0.75 eccentric factor in the activation model | Accentuated eccentric loading in the bench press — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8822896/ |

## Standard references (not fetched in this session)

Cited in the catalogue by name; the numbers taken from them are textbook
values the author is confident of, but they were not re-verified here.

- Perry J, Burnfield JM. *Gait Analysis*, 2nd ed. — gait-cycle muscle timing
  (vasti in loading response, gluteus maximus terminal swing → loading,
  plantarflexors mid → terminal stance, tibialis anterior initial contact and
  swing, iliopsoas pre-swing → initial swing) and joint-angle landmarks.
- Hug F, Dorel S 2009. *Electromyographic analysis of pedaling: a review.*
  J Electromyogr Kinesiol — crank-angle activation ranges (gluteus maximus
  ~340–130°, vasti ~300–130°, hamstrings ~30–220°, gastrocnemius ~30–270°,
  soleus ~340–180°, tibialis anterior in recovery).
- Kleshnev V 2016. *The Biomechanics of Rowing* — catch/drive/finish/recovery
  sequence (legs → trunk → arms) and drive:recovery ≈ 1:2.
- Schoenfeld BJ 2010. *Squatting kinematics and kinetics…* JSCR — trunk lean
  35–45° in the high-bar squat; Escamilla RF et al. 2000 (MSSE) — deadlift
  trunk angles, sumo 5–9° more upright.
- Contreras B et al. 2015 (J Appl Biomech) — hip thrust gluteus maximus EMG
  above squat and deadlift.
- Lake JP, Lauder MA 2012 (JSCR); Zebis MK et al. 2013 (BJSM) — kettlebell
  swing hip-extension velocity and semitendinosus bias.
- Ekstrom RA et al. 2007 (JOSPT); Distefano LJ et al. 2009 (JOSPT); Boren K
  et al. 2011 (IJSPT) — core/hip rehabilitation exercise EMG (bridge, side
  bridge, bird dog, clamshell, step-up, lunge).
- Escamilla RF et al. 2006 (JOSPT) — abdominal exercise EMG; McGill SM 2007
  *Low Back Disorders* — curl-up, side bridge, bird dog, and the spinal-load
  argument against loaded rotation for disc patients.
- Calatayud J et al. 2015 (JSCR) — push-up vs bench press EMG equivalence;
  Saeterbakken AH, Fimland MS 2013 (JSCR) — standing vs seated presses.
- Reinold MM et al. 2004 (JOSPT) — rotator-cuff external rotation exercises.
- Kolber MJ et al. 2010 (JSCR) — resistance-training shoulder injury
  patterns (dips, behind-neck, wide bench).
- Neumann DA 2017 *Kinesiology of the Musculoskeletal System*; NSCA
  *Essentials of Strength Training and Conditioning* 4th ed.; ACE Exercise
  Library; ExRx.net — technique cues and muscle roles.
